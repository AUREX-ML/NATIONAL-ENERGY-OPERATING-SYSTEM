# Session 2 — Public Infrastructure, Procurement & Institutional Delivery

## Purpose

Define how national DERMS infrastructure is procured, delivered and handed into sustainable institutional ownership without creating proprietary lock-in, abandoned assets or unfunded lifecycle obligations.

## Public Infrastructure Lifecycle

```text
PUBLIC NEED
    ↓
REQUIREMENTS
    ↓
ARCHITECTURE & STANDARDS
    ↓
PROCUREMENT
    ↓
DELIVERY
    ↓
ACCEPTANCE
    ↓
OPERATIONS HANDOVER
    ↓
LIFECYCLE MANAGEMENT
```

## Procurement Principle

Procurement begins with outcomes and requirements rather than a preferred vendor or product.

Specifications should address:

- Open interfaces
- Interoperability
- Data ownership and portability
- Cybersecurity
- Conformance
- Service levels
- Documentation
- Training
- Source-code and licensing considerations where applicable
- Maintenance
- Spares
- Upgrade paths
- Exit and migration
- Total lifecycle cost

## Institutional Ownership

Every delivered capability requires explicit accountability for:

- Asset ownership
- Platform ownership
- Operations
- Cybersecurity
- Data stewardship
- Maintenance
- Funding
- Vendor management
- Regulatory obligations

## Acceptance

Project completion does not equal operational acceptance.

Acceptance should require evidence that the system:

- Meets specified requirements
- Has passed technical and security tests
- Is documented
- Has trained operators
- Has support arrangements
- Has backup and recovery procedures
- Has an asset/configuration baseline
- Can be maintained after the implementation team leaves

## Anti-Lock-In Architecture

Public infrastructure should avoid unnecessary dependence on a single OEM, cloud provider, protocol, integrator or proprietary data model.

Where dependency is unavoidable, document its risk, owner, mitigation and exit path.

## Lifecycle Funding

Capital expenditure without operating expenditure creates stranded infrastructure. Funding plans must therefore account for:

- Connectivity
- Hosting
- Maintenance
- Field service
- Software lifecycle
- Cybersecurity
- Training
- Replacement
- Support

## Delivery Principle

The successful public project is not the one that installs the most equipment. It is the one that creates a capability institutions can sustainably own, operate, secure, maintain and improve.
