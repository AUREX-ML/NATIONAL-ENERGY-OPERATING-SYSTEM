# Session 3 — National Operations, Service Delivery & Lifecycle Management

## Purpose

Define how deployed DERMS infrastructure becomes a continuously operated national service rather than a collection of installed assets.

## Operations Model

```text
ASSETS
  ↓
PLATFORM CAPABILITIES
  ↓
OPERATIONAL SERVICES
  ↓
SERVICE LEVELS
  ↓
USERS / STAKEHOLDERS
```

## Core Operational Functions

National operations include:

- Service monitoring
- Incident management
- Problem management
- Change management
- Configuration management
- Capacity management
- Availability management
- Backup and recovery
- Business continuity
- Field service
- Maintenance
- Release management
- Vendor coordination
- Cybersecurity operations

## Service Ownership

Every production service requires:

- Service owner
- Technical owner
- Users/customers
- Dependencies
- Service level objectives
- Monitoring
- Escalation
- Support model
- Recovery objectives

## Incident Lifecycle

```text
DETECT → TRIAGE → CONTAIN → RESTORE → INVESTIGATE → CORRECT → LEARN
```

Incident records should preserve severity, affected services/sites, timeline, decisions, root cause where established, corrective actions and closure evidence.

## Change Management

Changes to production infrastructure require proportionate assessment of:

- Scope
- Risk
- Dependencies
- Security
- Rollback
- Testing
- Approval
- Deployment window
- Verification

Emergency changes remain controlled and retrospectively reviewed.

## Lifecycle Management

Every component eventually requires upgrade, replacement or retirement. Lifecycle governance tracks:

- Hardware age
- Firmware/software versions
- Support status
- Vulnerabilities
- Spare availability
- End-of-life dates
- Migration plans
- Decommissioning evidence

## Field Operations

National digital infrastructure still depends on physical sites. Field-service architecture therefore requires regional coverage, qualified personnel, tools, spares, safety procedures, dispatch, escalation and evidence capture.

## Continuity

The system must preserve safe local operation during failures of higher-level communications or coordination services. Recovery objectives are defined by service criticality rather than convenience.

## Operational Learning

```text
INCIDENT / MAINTENANCE / CHANGE
          ↓
LESSON
          ↓
RUNBOOK / STANDARD / PLATFORM IMPROVEMENT
          ↓
FUTURE DEPLOYMENTS
```
