# People Authority Matrix

## Purpose

This matrix establishes the principle that material People decisions require defined authority and appropriate independent approval.

> Final monetary thresholds and legal approval requirements should align with the organization's formal Delegation of Authority and applicable law.

| Decision | Initiates | Reviews / Controls | Final Authority at Maturity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Workforce plan | Functional leadership | People + Finance | CEO / approved planning authority |
| Create position | Manager / function lead | People + Finance | Authorized budget authority |
| Recruit | Hiring manager | People | Authorized manager |
| Employment offer | Hiring manager | People + Finance | Authorized hiring authority |
| Compensation offer/change | Manager | People + Finance | Authorized compensation authority |
| Promotion | Manager | People / calibration | Authorized leadership |
| Variable compensation | Manager | People + Finance | Authorized leadership |
| Executive compensation | Governance process | Appropriate independent review | Board / governing authority |
| Training authorization | Manager / function | People / safety / technical owner as relevant | Authorized role owner |
| Critical system access | Manager / system owner | Security + role/competency controls | Authorized system owner |
| Formal discipline | Manager | People + legal input where required | Authorized management |
| Termination | Manager | People + legal/process controls | Authorized management |
| CEO appointment/removal | Governance | Appropriate governance process | Board / governing authority |
| Policy exception | Requesting authority | People + risk/legal as relevant | Designated exception authority |

## No Self-Approval

No individual should possess unrestricted authority to approve material personal benefits or changes for themselves, including salary, bonus, equity, promotion, or equivalent exceptions.

## Authority Principles

1. Accountability precedes authority.
2. Authority is proportionate to role, competence, and consequence.
3. Higher authority carries greater accountability.
4. Critical decisions require traceable approval.
5. Conflicts of interest require disclosure and independent treatment.
6. Temporary authority has explicit scope and expiry.
7. Obsolete authority is removed when roles change.
8. Founder and executive decisions become progressively subject to institutional governance as the organization matures.
