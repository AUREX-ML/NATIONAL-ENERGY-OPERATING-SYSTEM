# Organizational Design

## Principle

Organizational structure exists to provide the capabilities required by the mission—not to manufacture titles or hierarchy.

## Design Sequence

```text
Mission
  ↓
Strategy
  ↓
Required Outcomes
  ↓
Capabilities
  ↓
Functions
  ↓
Roles
  ↓
Positions
  ↓
People
```

## Design Criteria

Structure should optimize for:

- clear accountability;
- fast legitimate decisions;
- appropriate specialization;
- manageable coordination cost;
- safe separation of conflicting duties;
- continuity of critical capabilities;
- scalability across facilities and regions.

## Functional Evolution

Early stages may combine responsibilities around a small founding team. As consequence and workload increase, capabilities progressively separate into areas such as engineering, product, operations, security, finance, commercial, people, risk, and governance.

## Organizational Data

The organization should maintain authoritative records for functions, teams, roles, positions, managers, and reporting lines. The organization chart should increasingly become an output of authoritative workforce data.

## Anti-Pattern

Do not create hierarchy before the underlying work requires it. A title does not create capability, authority, or accountability by itself.
