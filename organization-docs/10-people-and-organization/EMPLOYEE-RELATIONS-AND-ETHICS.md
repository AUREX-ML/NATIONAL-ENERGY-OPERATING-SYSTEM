# Employee Relations & Ethics

## Purpose

This framework governs how organizational power is exercised between people and how concerns, conflicts, misconduct, and grievances are handled.

## Code of Conduct Baseline

People acting on behalf of the organization are expected to demonstrate integrity, respect, professional competence, safety, confidentiality, fairness, accountability, and lawful conduct.

Seniority increases responsibility; it does not create exemptions.

## Ethical Hierarchy

```text
Safety
  ↓
Law
  ↓
Ethics
  ↓
Professional Duty
  ↓
Organizational Policy
  ↓
Commercial Interest
```

## Speak-Up

Good-faith concerns involving safety, fraud, corruption, harassment, security, financial misconduct, control failures, legal violations, or ethical issues must have legitimate reporting routes without inappropriate retaliation.

Serious concerns must be capable of bypassing ordinary management when the concern involves that management.

## Fair Investigation

```text
Allegation
  ↓
Scope
  ↓
Evidence
  ↓
Interviews / Response
  ↓
Analysis
  ↓
Finding
  ↓
Proportionate Action
```

An allegation does not create automatic guilt. An unsubstantiated complaint does not automatically mean the reporter acted dishonestly.

## Conflicts of Interest

```text
Identify → Disclose → Assess → Manage
```

Management options may include recusal, independent approval, monitoring, restriction, divestment, or prohibition depending on severity.

## Integrity Boundaries

Bribery, corruption, deliberate fraud, malicious system abuse, and deliberate falsification of safety or operational evidence are incompatible with legitimate organizational success.

## Founder and Executive Accountability

Serious allegations involving senior leadership must be capable of independent escalation. Founder or executive status does not place an individual above organizational conduct standards or legitimate governance.
