# Research & Innovation Architecture

## Purpose

This document defines how the institution converts strategically important uncertainty into governed research, evidence, decisions, standards, products and operational capability.

> Research exists to reduce uncertainty sufficiently for better decisions—not to produce activity for its own sake.

## Research Architecture

```text
Mission → Strategy → Research Agenda → Programs → Questions → Experiments → Evidence → Decisions → Transfer → Field Learning
```

## Research Classes

- Exploratory research — discovers poorly understood problem spaces.
- Descriptive research — characterizes systems and behavior.
- Comparative research — evaluates alternatives.
- Experimental research — measures effects of controlled changes.
- Validation research — determines whether requirements are met.
- Field research — tests behavior under real operating conditions.

## Research Programs

Long-term programs may include DER interoperability, facility energy management, edge computing, digital twins, multi-facility coordination, DER aggregation, flexibility, VPPs, forecasting and AI, grid interaction, energy markets, and cyber-physical resilience.

Programs are research portfolios, not additional organizational domains.

## Research Question Model

Every consequential research effort should define:

- research ID and owner;
- question and rationale;
- hypothesis;
- decision the research is intended to enable;
- method and evidence requirements;
- risks and dependencies;
- success/failure criteria;
- expected transfer path.

## Technology Readiness

Technology readiness and permission to progress are distinct. Maturity may move through concept, simulation, laboratory proof, controlled field validation, production qualification and scale qualification.

Claims must identify the actual evidence level: **concept, hypothesis, simulated, lab-validated, field-validated, or production-proven**.

## Research Portfolio

The portfolio records program, question, strategic value, uncertainty, consequence, TRL, owner, cost, status, evidence and next gate. Work should balance core uncertainties, adjacent capabilities and frontier research while prioritizing foundational unknowns.

## Research Prioritization

Priority is driven by strategic value, uncertainty reduction, consequence and decision urgency, adjusted for cost, time, dependencies and organizational capacity.

## Transfer Paths

Validated research must end with an explicit disposition:

- architecture / ADR;
- product requirement;
- standard / RFC;
- operational procedure or runbook;
- knowledge base;
- continued research; or
- no further action.

## Research Debt

Track untested assumptions, unreproduced results, unvalidated architecture, missing field evidence, stale benchmarks, uncertified integrations and unsupported scale claims.

## Core Laws

1. Research follows strategically important uncertainty rather than novelty alone.
2. Every consequential question has an accountable owner.
3. Claims remain hypotheses until supported by appropriate evidence.
4. Technology maturity does not itself grant production authority.
5. Evidence requirements increase with consequence and scale.
6. Negative and inconclusive results remain institutional knowledge.
7. Completed research ends with an explicit knowledge-transfer decision.
8. Research must remain traceable into the decisions and capabilities it influences.
