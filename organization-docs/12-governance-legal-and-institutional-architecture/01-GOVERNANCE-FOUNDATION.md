# Governance Foundation & Institutional Identity

## Purpose

This document defines the institutional foundation of the organization: its purpose, governance layers, decision rights, founder transition, role architecture, and institutional boundaries.

> **Master Governance Law:** The organization must be capable of exercising clear authority without concentrating undocumented power in individuals, collaborating openly without surrendering institutional accountability, and growing from a founder-led project into a durable institution whose mission, assets, decisions, and responsibilities remain understandable regardless of who currently occupies its roles.

## Institutional Identity

Three identities remain distinct:

- **Founder** — the individual who initiates and initially stewards the organization.
- **Organization** — the legal and institutional entity pursuing the mission.
- **Infrastructure / Product** — the National DERMS and related digital-energy systems developed and operated by the organization.

The founder is not the product. The product is not the legal organization. The organization must become larger than any individual contributor.

## Institutional Purpose

The organization exists to enable energy stakeholders to observe, integrate, coordinate, and progressively manage distributed energy infrastructure through open, interoperable, and dependable digital systems.

## Governance Hierarchy

A mature governance hierarchy may develop as follows:

```text
Owners / Members
      ↓
Governing Board
      ↓
Executive Leadership
      ↓
Management
      ↓
Engineering / Operations / Business
      ↓
Projects & Services
```

Governance complexity must grow with consequence rather than precede it unnecessarily.

## Founder Transition

Early-stage founder authority may span vision, strategy, product, architecture, capital, partnerships, and hiring. Over time those responsibilities should become explicit organizational functions.

The founder's long-term role is to establish the mission, architecture, culture, governance, and leadership system through which the organization can continue executing beyond one individual.

```text
Builder
  ↓
Founder
  ↓
Organizational Architect
  ↓
Institutional Steward
```

## Governance vs Management

- **Governance** defines mission stewardship, oversight, reserved matters, executive accountability, major capital decisions, and enterprise risk oversight.
- **Management** executes strategy within delegated authority.
- **Operations** make day-to-day decisions within defined scope and controls.

The governing body should not become the engineering department.

## Decision Rights

Every material organizational decision should have identifiable authority. Relevant domains include:

- Mission and strategy
- Technology architecture
- Production deployment
- Safety
- Cybersecurity
- Finance
- Commercial contracts
- Regulatory commitments
- People decisions
- Energy operations

Authority must be bounded by scope, limits, conditions, and escalation thresholds.

> **Authority and accountability travel together.**

## Organizational Functions

The institution may eventually require distinct functions for:

- Governance
- Executive leadership
- Engineering
- Product
- Energy systems
- Operations
- Cybersecurity
- Finance
- Legal and compliance
- Commercial
- Partnerships
- People and talent

One person may initially perform several functions, but the functions themselves remain conceptually separate.

## Role Before Person

Roles should be defined independently of current occupants so responsibility can transfer without redesigning the organization.

Critical roles should define:

- Purpose
- Accountability
- Decision rights
- Hard boundaries
- Interfaces
- Competencies
- Measures
- Escalation

## Institutional Independence

The organization must progressively ensure that project survival does not depend on founder availability.

Required mechanisms include:

- Documentation
- Governance
- Source control
- Access management
- Succession
- Financial controls
- Operating procedures
- Institutional knowledge

## Open-Source Governance

Corporate governance and open-source governance are distinct but coordinated.

Open-source roles may include users, contributors, maintainers, committers, technical leads, and steering structures. Participation can be open while authority is earned through demonstrated responsibility.

Contribution governance should define:

- How to contribute
- Review process
- Coding and testing standards
- Security process
- Licensing terms
- Maintainer authority

## Technical Governance

Material technical decisions should follow:

```text
Proposal
  ↓
Technical Review
  ↓
Decision
  ↓
Architecture Decision Record
  ↓
Implementation
```

Governance effort should increase for decisions affecting canonical data models, public APIs, control architecture, security boundaries, safety, licensing, or institutional interoperability.

## Conflict of Interest

Material conflicts should be disclosed, assessed, and governed. Recusal should be used where necessary.

## Transparency

Publish information that improves trust, interoperability, community participation, and accountability while protecting legitimate security, privacy, commercial confidentiality, and sensitive infrastructure information.

## Records and Policy Hierarchy

Material decisions should leave durable records including owner, date, rationale, and outcome.

Policy hierarchy:

```text
Founding / Constitutional Documents
            ↓
Governance Policies
            ↓
Organizational Policies
            ↓
Standards
            ↓
Procedures
            ↓
Work Instructions
```

## Institutional Boundaries

The organization is not automatically the regulator, utility, generator, transmission operator, distribution operator, retailer, manufacturer, EPC contractor, or physical asset owner simply because its infrastructure enables coordination.

Its enduring role is to build and operate the open digital coordination layer that enables independent energy stakeholders to work together.

## Governance Laws

1. Founder, organization, and infrastructure are distinct identities with distinct rights and responsibilities.
2. The organization exists to steward its mission beyond any individual person.
3. Authority is explicitly assigned, bounded, delegated, and accompanied by accountability.
4. Governance defines decision rights; management executes within those rights.
5. Organizational functions are defined independently of the people temporarily performing them.
6. Governance complexity grows with organizational consequence.
7. Open-source governance and corporate governance are distinct but coordinated systems.
8. Material technical, financial, operational, and institutional decisions leave durable records.
9. Experimentation remains easy while production authority remains controlled.
10. The organization does not assume statutory or operational roles merely because its software can technically support them.

## Definition of Done

The governance foundation is complete when the institution can clearly answer:

1. What institution are we building?
2. What is its purpose?
3. What belongs to the founder versus the organization?
4. What belongs to the organization versus the infrastructure?
5. Who makes strategic decisions?
6. Who makes technical decisions?
7. Who controls operational authority?
8. How is authority delegated?
9. Which decisions require higher approval?
10. How are important decisions recorded?
11. How does open-source participation work?
12. How are conflicts handled?
13. How does the organization remain operable beyond one person?
14. What institutional roles explicitly do not belong to us?
