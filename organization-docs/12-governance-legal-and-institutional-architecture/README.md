# 12 — Governance, Legal & Institutional Architecture

This module defines how the organization becomes a durable institution with clear ownership, legitimate authority, regulatory alignment, accountable decision-making, risk controls, stakeholder governance, and continuity beyond any individual person.

> **Master Institutional Law:** The organization shall distribute authority according to competence, legitimate mandate and consequence; preserve accountability through explicit decision rights, controls and evidence; protect stakeholder interests without confusing them with operational authority; and progressively transfer critical knowledge and capability from individuals into durable institutional systems so that the mission can outlive any particular founder, executive, employee, investor or contributor.

## Institutional Architecture

```text
Mission
  ↓
Legal Entity
  ↓
Ownership & IP
  ↓
Governance
  ↓
Regulatory Authority
  ↓
Risk & Controls
  ↓
Stakeholder Accountability
  ↓
Institutional Continuity
```

## Core Commitments

- Founder, legal entity, infrastructure, and open-source ecosystem are distinct identities.
- Ownership, governance, executive management, and operational authority remain separate.
- Technical capability never creates legal or regulatory authority by itself.
- Authority is explicit, bounded, delegated, and paired with accountability.
- Material risks have accountable owners, controls, evidence, and escalation paths.
- Stakeholder interest does not automatically create decision rights.
- Critical knowledge and authority progressively move from individuals into institutional systems.
- Governance grows with consequence without unnecessarily slowing experimentation.
