# Partnership Governance & Ecosystem Operations

## Purpose

This document turns ecosystem strategy into a repeatable institutional operating system.

> A partnership becomes institutional capability only when its purpose, ownership, obligations, interfaces, performance, risks, decisions, and institutional history remain understandable and governable beyond the individuals who created it.

## Relationship Ownership

Every material relationship requires an accountable internal owner responsible for purpose, communication, commitments, performance, escalation, documentation, and review.

Critical relationships should also have secondary ownership and institutional records to avoid key-person dependency.

## Qualification

Evaluate prospective relationships for:

- strategic fit;
- mission fit;
- capability fit;
- economic value;
- institutional value;
- technical fit;
- reputation;
- execution capacity;
- risk;
- timing.

## Due Diligence

Diligence should increase with risk. Areas may include legal existence, ownership, reputation, financial capacity, technical capability, delivery history, security, privacy, compliance, conflicts, anti-corruption controls, and insurance where required.

## Agreement Architecture

Use the instrument appropriate to the relationship:

```text
NDA                    → confidentiality
MOU                    → cooperation framework
SERVICE AGREEMENT      → commercial service
DATA-SHARING AGREEMENT → data rights
RESEARCH AGREEMENT     → research/IP/publication
PARTNER AGREEMENT      → ecosystem participation
SLA                    → service commitments
CONSORTIUM AGREEMENT   → joint delivery
INVESTMENT AGREEMENT   → capital/governance
```

Maintain an agreement register containing ownership, effective dates, expiry, renewal, financial obligations, data obligations, security obligations, key rights, termination, and status.

## Commitment Authority

Define who may commit the organization regarding pricing, exclusivity, data rights, IP, service levels, capital, public statements, technical control, and legal liability.

Enthusiasm is not signing authority.

## Access Governance

Partner access follows least privilege:

```text
PARTNER ROLE
     ↓
REQUIRED ACCESS
     ↓
APPROVAL
     ↓
PROVISION
     ↓
MONITOR
     ↓
REVIEW
     ↓
REVOKE
```

Prefer named identities, strong authentication, role-based authorization, and audit logging over shared credentials.

## Certification Records

For certified participants record participant, certification type, scope, version, test results, issue date, expiry, restrictions, and status.

Certification must be evidence-based, reviewable, renewable, and withdrawable.

## Institutional CRM

Store relationship history systematically, including contacts, meetings, decisions, documents, commitments, open questions, opportunities, risks, and next actions.

Important meetings should capture purpose, participants, key discussion, decisions, commitments, owners, deadlines, and next actions.

## Performance Management

Partner scorecards may cover:

- delivery;
- quality;
- reliability;
- security;
- economics;
- strategic contribution;
- collaboration;
- risk.

Use operational evidence rather than personal relationships alone.

## Ecosystem KPIs

Potential metrics include active partners, certified participants, supported OEMs, certified devices, regional coverage, external contributors, partner-sourced deployments, partner-sourced revenue, delivery quality, research partnerships, and capital mobilized.

Ecosystem quality is more important than raw participant count.

Measure concentration across revenue, devices, deployments, connectivity, cloud, capital, technical capability, and geography where relevant.

## Substitutability Test

For critical partners ask:

```text
IF THIS PARTNER DISAPPEARS TOMORROW:

Can we continue?
How long?
What breaks?
Who can replace them?
What data or access must transfer?
```

## Incident Governance

Partner incidents may include service failure, cyber incidents, data breaches, safety events, compliance failures, financial distress, or misconduct.


Poor performance should trigger documented corrective action. Repeated or severe failure may lead to suspension or termination.

## Renewal and Expansion

Renewal must review performance, strategic fit, economics, risk, alternatives, dependency, and future need.

Successful relationships may expand across sites, regions, products, integrations, market development, or research, but expansion still requires risk review.

## Exit

Partnership exit must protect customers, data, systems, IP, operations, finance, and reputation.

Offboarding should cover notice, final obligations, data export/deletion, access revocation, asset return, settlement, customer transition, migration, certification withdrawal, and record closure.

## Domain Integration

Partnership governance connects directly to strategy, product, operations, finance, enterprise risk, security, people, governance, data, and open-source stewardship.

External commitments require internal authority. External dependencies require internal risk controls. External access requires security controls. External capability should complement internal capability.

## Partnership Governance Laws

1. Material external relationships have documented purpose, ownership, obligations, and history.
2. Every critical relationship has continuity beyond its original owner.
3. Partnership opportunities are qualified before substantial resources are committed.
4. Due diligence increases with consequence.
5. Agreements use structures appropriate to the relationship.
6. External representation does not create unauthorized commitment authority.
7. Partner access follows least privilege.
8. Certification is evidence-based and withdrawable.
9. Performance is measured through real operating outcomes.
10. Critical dependencies have alternatives, continuity mechanisms, or consciously accepted risks.
11. Renewal and expansion are governance decisions.
12. Exit protects customers, systems, data, IP, and operations.
13. Public claims accurately represent relationship status.
14. Conflicts of interest and corruption risks are explicitly controlled.
15. Platform defensibility should arise from usefulness, trust, interoperability, standards, and network effects rather than artificial lock-in.

## Domain 14 Definition of Done

Domain 14 is operationally mature when the organization can identify which external institutions matter, define reciprocal value, assign accountable relationship owners, qualify and diligence partners, select appropriate agreements, control external commitments and system access, certify ecosystem participants, measure partner performance, monitor concentration and dependency, manage incidents, renew or terminate relationships safely, govern capital and strategic alliances, preserve interoperability and neutrality, and scale ecosystem capability faster than internal headcount alone.

## Master Partnership & Ecosystem Law

The organization shall scale beyond its institutional boundaries through governed collaboration: identifying the external capabilities required by the mission, building reciprocal relationships with legitimate stakeholders, converting those relationships into documented and accountable institutional interfaces, protecting interoperability, neutrality, security and public integrity, measuring partnership value through real operating outcomes, managing dependencies and exits deliberately, and creating an increasingly self-sustaining ecosystem capable of extending energy infrastructure, technical capability, capital, knowledge and economic opportunity beyond what the organization could build alone.
