# Risk Governance Framework

## Purpose

This framework defines accountability, oversight, challenge, assurance, escalation, and decision rights for enterprise risk management.

## Governance Architecture

```text
Governing Body
      ↓
Executive Management
      ↓
Risk Owners
      ↓
Control Owners
      ↓
Operations
      ↓
Evidence / Indicators
      ↓
Assurance
      ↓
Management & Governance Review
```

## Governing Body

At institutional maturity, the governing body:

- approves risk appetite;
- oversees top enterprise risks;
- challenges executive management;
- reviews material tolerance breaches;
- oversees systemic safety, cybersecurity, financial, regulatory, and resilience exposure;
- reviews major assurance findings;
- holds executive management accountable for remediation.

## Executive Management

Executive management is responsible for operating the enterprise risk-management system, allocating resources, resolving cross-functional exposures, accepting risk within delegated authority, and escalating matters beyond its authority.

## Risk Owners

Every material risk has one accountable owner with sufficient authority to manage its exposure. The owner is accountable for assessment, controls, treatment, indicators, escalation, and review.

## Control Owners

Control owners are responsible for implementing, operating, evidencing, monitoring, and maintaining assigned controls.

## Three Lines

### First Line — Own and Operate
Engineering, Operations, Product, Commercial, Delivery, Finance Operations, and partners where contractually applicable.

### Second Line — Challenge and Govern
Risk, Compliance, Security Governance, Safety Governance, Legal, Data Governance, and Financial Control.

### Third Line — Independent Assurance
Internal audit, external assurance, independent technical review, or other appropriately independent assessment.

## Governance Cadence

Conceptual cadence:

- **Continuous / event-driven:** critical KRIs and incidents.
- **Weekly:** material operational risk signals where scale requires it.
- **Monthly:** management risk review.
- **Quarterly:** enterprise risk review.
- **Periodic:** governing-body oversight.
- **Annual:** framework and appetite review.

Actual cadence shall remain proportional to scale and consequence.

## Event-Driven Review

Immediate or accelerated review may be triggered by:

- major incident;
- material architecture change;
- new market or jurisdiction;
- new regulatory requirement;
- significant financing;
- major customer or supplier dependency;
- increased DER control authority;
- significant safety event;
- material cybersecurity event;
- major assurance finding.

## Escalation

Normal escalation:

```text
Control Owner
    ↓
Risk Owner
    ↓
Functional Leadership
    ↓
Executive Management
    ↓
Governing Body
```

Critical conditions may bypass normal hierarchy where delay would materially increase harm.

## Assurance

Assurance intensity follows consequence. High-consequence systems should progressively move from management monitoring to second-line review and appropriate independent assurance.

Findings require severity, risk linkage, accountable ownership, remediation, target date, verification, and formal closure.

## Risk Culture

The organization expects material bad news to become visible early. Good-faith escalation of risk, incidents, near misses, and control weaknesses should be treated as an input to improvement rather than suppressed to preserve appearances.

## Innovation

Risk governance must enable bounded experimentation:

```text
Idea
 ↓
Risk Assessment
 ↓
Simulation
 ↓
Laboratory
 ↓
Bounded Pilot
 ↓
Evidence
 ↓
Scale
```

Risk management is therefore an enabling architecture for responsible innovation.
