# Risk Authority Matrix

## Purpose

The Risk Authority Matrix defines who may accept, escalate, or prohibit material risk decisions.

Authority must increase with residual exposure and consequence.

## Conceptual Authority Model

| Decision | Operating Team | Risk Owner / Functional Lead | Executive Management | Governing Body |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Manage low residual risk within tolerance | ✓ | Oversight |  |  |
| Accept low residual risk | ✓ | ✓ |  |  |
| Accept moderate residual risk | Recommend | ✓ | Oversight |  |
| Accept high residual risk | ✕ | Recommend | ✓ | Oversight / approval where material |
| Accept critical / existential residual risk | ✕ | ✕ | Recommend | ✓ where legally permissible |
| Approve material change to risk appetite | ✕ | Recommend | Recommend | ✓ |
| Approve material regulatory-market entry | ✕ | Recommend | ✓ / Recommend | Oversight where material |
| Approve systemic security exception | ✕ | Recommend | ✓ | Oversight where material |
| Override hard safety boundary | ✕ | ✕ | ✕ | ✕ |
| Authorize bribery, fraud, corruption, or knowingly unlawful conduct | ✕ | ✕ | ✕ | ✕ |

## Principles

1. Authority is based on **residual** risk, not merely inherent risk.
2. Risk owners cannot delegate accountability merely by delegating control operation.
3. A higher management level does not automatically have authority to override a hard boundary.
4. Where law or professional regulation requires a specific qualified authority, internal hierarchy cannot substitute for that requirement.
5. Material exceptions must be documented, risk-assessed, approved, time-bounded where appropriate, and supported by compensating controls.
6. Critical conditions may require immediate containment before normal approval processes are completed.

## Reserved Risk Matters

At institutional maturity, the following may become governance-reserved matters:

- material change in enterprise risk appetite;
- entry into materially new regulated activities;
- acceptance of existential financial exposure;
- large-scale infrastructure-control authority;
- systemic safety or cybersecurity exceptions;
- mission-changing acquisitions or partnerships;
- major related-party transactions;
- material deviations from approved governance policy.

## Evolution

This matrix is intentionally principle-based during early organizational maturity. Quantitative delegation limits should be added as financial scale, staffing, regulatory status, and deployment consequence become measurable.
