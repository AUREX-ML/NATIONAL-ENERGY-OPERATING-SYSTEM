# Risk Management Framework

## Purpose

This framework defines the institutional system for managing material uncertainty across the National Distributed Energy Resource Management System initiative.

Risk management is embedded across strategy, engineering, operations, product, finance, commercial activity, cybersecurity, safety, compliance, people, supply chain, partnerships, and governance.

## Risk Statements

Material risks should be expressed using a cause-event-consequence structure:

> Because **[cause]**, there is a risk that **[event]** occurs, resulting in **[consequence]**.

This structure enables meaningful ownership, controls, indicators, and treatment.

## Risk Assessment

Risks are evaluated on two levels:

### Inherent Risk

Exposure before considering the controls relied upon by the organization.

### Residual Risk

Exposure remaining after considering the effectiveness of applicable controls.

```text
Inherent Risk
      ↓
Controls
      ↓
Residual Risk
      ↓
Appetite / Tolerance
```

Control existence alone does not justify reducing assessed risk. Control design, operation, evidence, and testing must be considered.

## Risk Treatment

The organization may:

- **Avoid** — discontinue or decline the activity.
- **Reduce** — introduce or strengthen controls.
- **Transfer / Share** — allocate part of the exposure contractually, through insurance, or through an appropriate partner.
- **Accept** — consciously retain residual exposure within authorized authority.

Risk acceptance is an explicit decision, not the absence of action.

## Controls

Controls may be preventive, detective, corrective, or recovery-oriented.

For high-consequence risks, defense in depth should be used so that failure of a single control does not create catastrophic exposure.

A control is considered dependable only when its design is appropriate, it operates as intended, evidence exists, and proportionate testing supports that conclusion.

## Indicators

### KPI — Key Performance Indicator
Measures whether an objective is being achieved.

### KRI — Key Risk Indicator
Measures whether risk exposure may be increasing or decreasing.

### KCI — Key Control Indicator
Measures whether a material control is functioning as intended.

Indicators should have defined thresholds and corresponding actions. A red indicator must produce action or escalation rather than merely change dashboard appearance.

## Incident and Crisis Management

Incidents are classified according to consequence and urgency. Critical safety, cybersecurity, infrastructure, fraud, legal, or continuity events may bypass normal reporting hierarchy and trigger immediate escalation.

Every material incident should produce:

1. containment;
2. recovery;
3. evidence preservation;
4. root-cause analysis where appropriate;
5. corrective actions;
6. lessons learned;
7. reassessment of affected risks and controls.

## Business Continuity and Resilience

Critical services require defined recovery objectives, dependencies, backup strategies, recovery procedures, and periodic testing.

For distributed energy infrastructure, safe local operation should remain possible wherever technically appropriate when central services are unavailable.

## Cybersecurity

Digital control authority must be protected through identity, authorization, least privilege, secure communications, command validation, logging, monitoring, vulnerability management, device security, software supply-chain controls, and incident response.

## Safety

Human safety is a hard boundary. Optimization, revenue, availability, deadlines, customer pressure, or executive authority cannot override verified safety constraints.

Remote control capability must be constrained by validated operating limits, interlocks, fail-safe behavior, commissioning controls, and appropriate professional engineering responsibility.

## Regulatory, Legal and Compliance Risk

Technical capability does not itself authorize an activity. Regulatory obligations depend on jurisdiction, activity, assets, customers, and authority exercised.

Material expansion from monitoring to analytics, remote control, aggregation, market participation, regional DERMS, or national infrastructure requires reassessment of the regulatory perimeter.

## Three Lines

### First Line — Own and Operate
Engineering, Product, Operations, Commercial, Delivery, and other operating teams own risks created by their activities and operate controls.

### Second Line — Challenge and Govern
Risk, Compliance, Security Governance, Safety Governance, Legal, Financial Control, and Data Governance establish frameworks and independently challenge First Line management.

### Third Line — Assure
Internal audit and appropriate independent assurance assess whether governance, risk management, and controls are operating effectively.

## Risk Monitoring

Risk monitoring combines:

- enterprise risk ratings;
- trends;
- KRIs;
- KCIs;
- incidents and near misses;
- control deficiencies;
- assurance findings;
- regulatory change;
- architecture and deployment changes;
- concentration and dependency analysis.

## Review Triggers

Material risk reassessment occurs when there is a major incident, architecture change, new market, new regulation, large customer, significant financing, increased control authority, major supplier failure, material safety event, or significant change in deployment scale.

## Maturity Model

### Laboratory
Founder-led risk identification, basic safety, cybersecurity, licensing, and top-risk tracking.

### Facility Pilot
Structured register, defined owners, deployment controls, incident processes, and customer/data obligations.

### Commercial Facility EMS
Enterprise register, formal policies, KRIs/KCIs, executive review, continuity planning, and compliance ownership.

### Regional DERMS
Portfolio risk, automated risk telemetry, regulatory engagement, formal assurance, and cross-customer governance.

### National DERMS
Systemic infrastructure risk monitoring, institutional governance, independent assurance, national regulatory coordination, and real-time operational risk visibility.

## Master Principle

> **Observe → Understand → Coordinate → Control → Learn.**

The same philosophy used to manage distributed energy resources is applied to the institution itself.
