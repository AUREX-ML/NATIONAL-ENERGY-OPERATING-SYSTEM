# 09 — Risk Management

The **Risk Management** module defines how the organization identifies, assesses, owns, controls, monitors, escalates, assures, and learns from uncertainty across strategy, engineering, operations, finance, cybersecurity, safety, compliance, people, partners, and infrastructure.

> **Master Risk Law:** The organization may deliberately take risk in pursuit of its mission, but material risk must remain visible, owned, bounded, controlled, monitored, and accountable—and where the potential consequence becomes systemic, independent evidence must demonstrate that the institution is capable of carrying that responsibility.

## Core Principles

1. Risk management exists to enable responsible achievement of organizational objectives—not to eliminate uncertainty.
2. Every material risk has one accountable owner.
3. Risk-taking authority is bounded by appetite, tolerance, and hard limits.
4. Controls must be demonstrable mechanisms supported by evidence.
5. Human safety outranks optimization, availability, revenue, and schedule.
6. Digital control authority is limited by the organization's ability to authenticate, authorize, constrain, monitor, and safely execute it.
7. Central infrastructure failure should degrade capability rather than unnecessarily eliminate safe local operation.
8. Technical capability does not itself create regulatory, contractual, or professional authority.
9. Incidents, near misses, assurance findings, and control failures feed organizational learning.
10. Governance strength increases with authority, scale, interconnectedness, and consequence.

## Relationship to Other Organization Modules

The **Operating Model** defines how the organization works.

The **Financial Model** defines how it sustains and governs economic responsibility.

The **Risk Management** module defines how uncertainty and downside exposure are made visible, bounded, governed, and continuously reduced as the organization accepts greater infrastructure responsibility.
