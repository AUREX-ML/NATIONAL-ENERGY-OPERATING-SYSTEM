# Crisis Management, Emergency Operations & Incident Command

## Purpose

This document defines the authority, coordination, communication and decision structures used during material incidents affecting organizational or National DERMS operations.

## Incident Management Principle

During crisis conditions, authority and communication must become clearer—not more ambiguous.

```text
DETECT → CLASSIFY → DECLARE → COMMAND → STABILIZE → RECOVER → CLOSE → LEARN
```

## Severity Model

Incidents shall use a standardized severity framework based on safety, operational impact, affected users/assets, geographic scope, regulatory implications, financial consequence and recovery complexity.

Higher severity shall trigger stronger command, communication and governance requirements.

## Incident Command

Material incidents shall have explicitly assigned roles appropriate to scale, including:

- Incident Commander
- Operations/technical lead
- Security lead where relevant
- Communications lead
- Business/continuity lead
- Safety/field lead where relevant
- Executive liaison
- Scribe/decision-log owner

One person may hold multiple roles during early-stage operations, but responsibilities must remain explicit.

## Emergency Authority

Emergency authority shall define who may:

- declare a major incident
- isolate systems
- suspend unsafe operations
- initiate failover
- invoke continuity plans
- authorize emergency expenditure
- contact regulators or public authorities
- issue external communications
- approve return to normal

Emergency authority must be bounded, logged and reviewed after the event.

## Safety Priority

Protection of human safety and prevention of dangerous energy-system behavior outrank service restoration speed.

## Common Operating Picture

During significant incidents maintain a current view of:

- what happened
- what is affected
- current safety state
- current service state
- actions underway
- responsible owners
- dependencies/blockers
- decisions required
- next update time

## Situation Reports

Situation reports should be concise, time-stamped and decision-oriented. They shall distinguish confirmed facts, working hypotheses, unknowns and planned actions.

## Decision Log

Record consequential decisions with time, decision maker, available information, rationale, action and later outcome where relevant.

## Communications

Crisis communication shall be coordinated. Internal and external messages must be accurate, timely, appropriately authorized and consistent with legal/regulatory obligations.

Stakeholders may include employees, customers, utilities, suppliers, regulators, emergency services, investors, government institutions and the public depending on the event.

## Emergency Operations Coordination

For large or prolonged events, establish an Emergency Operations Center (physical or virtual) providing command coordination, common information, action tracking and shift handover.

## Cyber/Operational Coordination

Cyber incidents affecting energy operations require integrated security and operational command. Containment decisions shall consider their effect on physical operations, safety, telemetry and control.

## Field Operations

Where field intervention is necessary, remote command shall not instruct personnel to bypass electrical safety, lockout/tagout, site procedures or lawful emergency requirements.

## Long-Duration Incidents

Long incidents require shift structures, documented handovers, fatigue management, continuity of authority and preservation of decision context.

## Return to Normal

Incident closure shall require defined stabilization criteria and authorized transition from emergency command to normal operations. Restoration shall include monitoring for recurrence.

## Post-Incident Review

Significant incidents shall undergo review covering:

- timeline
- impact
- causes and contributing conditions
- control performance
- decisions
- communications
- recovery performance
- lessons
- corrective actions

The objective is institutional learning, not blame allocation.

## Corrective Actions

Actions require an owner, target date, status and evidence of completion. High-consequence corrective actions shall be retested where applicable.

## Repeated Incidents

Repeated incidents with materially identical causes indicate failure to learn and require escalating governance attention.

## Definition of Done

This control area is complete when incident severity is standardized, command roles and emergency authorities are explicit, safety remains dominant, material incidents maintain common operating pictures and decision logs, communications are governed, long incidents support handover, return-to-normal authority is clear, and significant incidents produce verified corrective actions.
