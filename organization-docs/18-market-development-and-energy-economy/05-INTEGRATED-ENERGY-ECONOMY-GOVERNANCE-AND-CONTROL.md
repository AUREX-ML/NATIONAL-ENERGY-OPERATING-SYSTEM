# Integrated Energy-Economy Governance & Control

## Purpose

Integrate physical energy, digital coordination, market, commercial and institutional systems into one governed operating architecture.

> Economic opportunity never overrides physical safety, lawful authority, customer rights, evidence requirements or institutional accountability.

## Integrated Architecture

```text
INSTITUTIONAL / REGULATORY GOVERNANCE
                 ↓
MARKET GOVERNANCE
                 ↓
COMMERCIAL & SETTLEMENT SYSTEM
                 ↓
NATIONAL / REGIONAL DERMS
                 ↓
FACILITY EMS
                 ↓
DERs + METERS + PHYSICAL GRID
```

Authority moves downward only where legitimate. Evidence and operational state move upward.

## Governing Hierarchy

```text
PHYSICAL SAFETY

GRID SECURITY

COMMERCIAL OPTIMIZATION
```

## Institutional Roles

The platform distinguishes technology provider, DERMS operator, facility operator, aggregator, VPP operator, utility, system operator, market operator, settlement operator and regulator. Technical capability does not automatically confer institutional authority.

## Authority Model

```text
LEGAL / CONTRACTUAL AUTHORITY
 ↓
INSTITUTIONAL APPROVAL
 ↓
SYSTEM ROLE
 ↓
TECHNICAL PERMISSION
```

API access is never treated as proof of authority.

## Separation of Duties

High-consequence activities should separate market-rule creation, participant qualification, transaction execution, meter validation, settlement calculation, settlement approval, payment authorization and audit where appropriate.

## Rule Hierarchy

```text
LAW / REGULATION
 ↓
MARKET RULES
 ↓
CONTRACT
 ↓
PRODUCT SPECIFICATION
 ↓
OPERATIONAL PROCEDURE
 ↓
MACHINE RULE
```

Code executes policy; it does not create legitimate policy by itself.

## Readiness Framework

Scale readiness should consider technology readiness (TRL), market readiness (MRL), commercial readiness (CRL), institutional readiness (IRL), operational readiness and security readiness.

A technically mature system may still be commercially or institutionally immature.

## Market Readiness Levels

- MRL-0 Concept
- MRL-1 Market hypothesis
- MRL-2 Customer discovery
- MRL-3 Technical validation
- MRL-4 Controlled pilot
- MRL-5 Commercial pilot
- MRL-6 Repeatable service
- MRL-7 Multi-site market operation
- MRL-8 Institutional market integration
- MRL-9 National / regional scale

## Risk Governance

The integrated risk framework covers technical, grid, market, commercial, financial, customer, regulatory, cybersecurity, data, partner, reputation and systemic risks.

## Failure Isolation

Higher-level market or cloud failure must not remove safe local operation. Blast radius should be constrained by facility, portfolio, region, service and market product.

A market halt is not a facility shutdown.

## Market Surveillance

Monitor abnormal pricing, false availability, repeated delivery shortfalls, meter anomalies, baseline gaming, duplicate claims and other integrity signals. Automated detection creates a signal, not a finding of guilt; consequential action requires governed review.

## Customer & Asset Protection

Customers require protection against unauthorized control, hidden commitments, unexplained settlement, excessive asset use and data misuse. Market optimization must respect warranties, maintenance, thermal limits, state-of-charge limits, cycle limits, degradation and safety.

## Economic Sustainability

```text
VALUE CREATED > TOTAL COST OF COORDINATION
```

over a viable horizon. Transaction costs, measurement, verification and settlement overhead must remain proportionate to the value being coordinated.

## KPI Integrity

Connected MW, qualified MW, available MW, committed MW, traded MW and delivered MW are separate metrics. Likewise, pilots, customers, contracted revenue and recurring revenue remain distinct.
