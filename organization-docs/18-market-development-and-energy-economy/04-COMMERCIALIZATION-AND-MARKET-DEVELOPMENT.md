# Commercialization & Market Development

## Purpose

Define how validated technical capability becomes measurable customer value, repeatable services, sustainable revenue and scalable market adoption.

## Commercialization Chain

```text
TECHNICAL CAPABILITY
 ↓
CUSTOMER PROBLEM
 ↓
VALUE PROPOSITION
 ↓
SERVICE
 ↓
COMMERCIAL OFFER
 ↓
CONTRACT
 ↓
DEPLOYMENT
 ↓
CUSTOMER OUTCOME
 ↓
REVENUE
 ↓
REPEATABLE BUSINESS MODEL
```

## Commercialization Stages

1. Research / prototype
2. Pilot
3. Paid pilot
4. First production customer
5. Repeatable customer deployment
6. Multi-site / fleet
7. Utility / institutional program
8. Market infrastructure
9. National / regional scale

## Customer Architecture

Distinguish the user, customer, buyer, payer, beneficiary and regulator. These roles may belong to different parties.

## Segment Qualification

Evaluate segments using problem severity, ability to pay, urgency, DER density, data/control access, procurement complexity, sales cycle, deployment cost, repeatability and strategic value.

## Service Productization

Move from custom engineering toward a common configurable platform:

```text
COMMON CORE
+
CONFIGURATION
+
STANDARD ADAPTERS
+
LIMITED EXTENSIONS
```

Customer requests should be classified as standard, configuration, extension, custom or reject.

## Commercial Models

Potential models include deployment fees, subscriptions, per-facility fees, per-asset fees, per-MW managed fees, managed-service fees, support contracts, transaction fees, value share and hybrids.

Infrastructure management and coordination can create recurring value before mature flexibility markets exist.

## Unit Economics

Track customer acquisition cost, implementation cost, recurring delivery cost, cost-to-serve, gross margin, contribution margin, contract value, retention, expansion and customer concentration.

Revenue alone is not evidence of economic sustainability.

## Pilot Discipline

Every pilot must state the uncertainty it is designed to remove. Progress should move toward paid validation rather than indefinite free pilots.

```text
RESEARCH
→ CO-FUNDED PILOT
→ PAID PILOT
→ PRODUCTION CONTRACT
```

## Partnership Architecture

Partners may include OEMs, EPCs, system integrators, ESCOs, telecom operators, cloud providers, universities, utilities, financial institutions and development institutions. Every partnership should define contribution, value exchange, customer ownership, data rights, support responsibilities, risk and revenue sharing.


## Commercial Integrity

Do not describe discussions as partnerships, pilots as customers, projected savings as delivered savings, or theoretical market size as obtainable revenue.

## Definition of Done

The organization can identify customer problems and buyers; segment markets; define services; price them; calculate deployment and recurring economics; run evidence-driven pilots; convert successful pilots into production; govern partnerships and contracts; track customer outcomes and retention; and expand only where repeatability and economics are demonstrated.
