# Financial Model

## National Distributed Energy Resource Management System

> **Financial thesis:** The organization converts capital into reusable coordination capability, converts that capability into measurable customer and infrastructure value, earns sustainable revenue by accepting defined operational responsibility, protects the resulting cash through disciplined financial governance, and reinvests surplus to expand the amount of energy infrastructure it can responsibly coordinate.

---

## 1. Purpose

This Financial Model defines how the organization creates, captures, finances, protects, measures, and reinvests economic value as it develops from Facility Energy Management into Regional and National DERMS infrastructure.

It is not a fixed price list or a speculative forecast. It is the financial operating architecture that connects the organization's mission, product roadmap, operating model, customer responsibility, capital requirements, and governance.

The model is designed around one central principle:

> **Financial strength must increase the organization's ability to place more energy infrastructure under trusted coordination without compromising reliability, openness, independence, or institutional continuity.**

---

## 2. Economic Engine

The organization does not primarily monetize software ownership. It monetizes defined responsibility for deploying, integrating, operating, supporting, and coordinating energy infrastructure.

The economic loop is:

```text
Capital
  ↓
Reusable Capability
  ↓
Facility Deployment
  ↓
Infrastructure Under Coordination
  ↓
Customer Value
  ↓
Revenue
  ↓
Contribution
  ↓
Cash
  ↓
Reserves + Reinvestment
  ↓
Greater Capability
  ↺
```

Primary revenue categories may include:

- assessment and engineering;
- integration and commissioning;
- deployment services;
- managed infrastructure services;
- operations and support;
- platform and coordination services;
- partner enablement and certification where appropriate;
- strategic or research programmes where explicitly funded.

Open-source software remains compatible with this model because economic value is created through integration, responsibility, reliability, coordination, operational capability, and infrastructure outcomes.

## 3. Pricing Architecture

Pricing should reflect the responsibility assumed rather than simply the amount of software delivered.

Relevant pricing drivers include:

- facility complexity;
- number and diversity of energy assets;
- integration complexity;
- deployment effort;
- level of operational authority;
- reliability requirements;
- support requirements;
- cybersecurity responsibility;
- field-service exposure;
- data and coordination requirements.

Pricing should distinguish between one-time deployment economics and recurring managed-service economics.

```text
Deployment Price
= Assessment
+ Engineering
+ Hardware where applicable
+ Integration
+ Installation
+ Commissioning

Recurring Price
= Monitoring
+ Platform Operations
+ Support
+ Maintenance
+ Security
+ Coordination Responsibility
```

Prices and commercial terms are determined from evidence and market conditions; this document does not prescribe arbitrary fixed prices.

## 4. Unit Economics

The facility is the first measurable economic unit.

### Deployment contribution

```text
Deployment Revenue
- Hardware
- Integration Engineering
- Installation
- Commissioning
- Travel / Logistics
- External Contractors
= Deployment Contribution
```

### Recurring contribution

```text
Managed-Service Revenue
- Cloud / Backend
- Monitoring
- Support
- Maintenance
- Field Service
- Security Operations
- Allocated Infrastructure Cost
= Recurring Contribution
```

Deployment economics and recurring economics must be measured separately.

Core unit-economics metrics include:

- deployment gross margin;
- recurring gross margin;
- cost to serve per facility;
- recurring contribution per facility;
- engineering hours per deployment;
- commissioning hours;
- deployment duration;
- support hours per facility per month;
- integration reuse percentage;
- customer acquisition cost;
- CAC payback;
- retention and expansion.

Custom engineering should progressively become reusable platform capability. If facility count grows without reductions in engineering, commissioning, and support effort per facility, scalability is constrained.

Strategic pilots and research deployments must be explicitly classified so their intentionally different economics do not corrupt commercial unit-economics data.

## 5. Cash Flow and Working Capital

Profitability does not guarantee liquidity.

Customer-specific expenditure should normally be financed through customer payments or an explicitly approved financing source rather than silently consuming unrestricted operating cash.

The organization should structure project cash flows around milestones where commercially and contractually appropriate.

```text
Contract / Mobilization
      ↓
Engineering / Procurement
      ↓
Installation
      ↓
Commissioning / Acceptance
      ↓
Final Settlement
```

For every material project, Finance should understand:

```text
Cumulative Project Spending
- Cumulative Cash Received
= Working-Capital Exposure
```

The maximum negative point represents the project's financing requirement.

Working-capital sources may include customer deposits, milestone payments, supplier credit, working-capital facilities, project finance, strategic funding, or other approved capital.

Hardware procurement should normally be funded before or at procurement when the equipment is expensive or customer-specific.

The organization must distinguish:

```text
Signed Contract
≠ Revenue Earned
≠ Invoice Issued
≠ Cash Received
```

Receivables should be actively monitored, including aging and expected collection dates.

A rolling cash forecast should provide visibility into upcoming obligations and liquidity requirements.

## 6. Funding Architecture

Different forms of capital finance different forms of risk.

Conceptual progression:

```text
Founder / Internal Capital
        ↓
Grants + Research Funding
        ↓
Pilot / Strategic Funding
        ↓
Customer Revenue
        ↓
Equity / Strategic Capital
        ↓
Working-Capital Finance
        ↓
Project / Infrastructure Finance
        ↓
Development / Institutional Finance
```

This is not a rigid chronological sequence. Multiple forms of capital may coexist.

### Capital matching

| Need | Appropriate capital categories |
|---|---|
| Early research | Founder capital, grants, research funding |
| Prototype development | Founder capital, grants, appropriate early equity |
| Strategic pilots | Customer, grant, research or strategic programme funding |
| Commercial Facility EMS | Customer revenue and appropriate company capital |
| Deployment timing gaps | Customer payments, supplier credit, working-capital finance |
| Core company expansion | Equity, retained earnings, strategic capital |
| Regional infrastructure | Revenue, project/development/strategic finance |
| Utility and national infrastructure | Utility, government, project, DFI and institutional finance as appropriate |

High-risk capital should reduce uncertainty. Lower-cost capital should increasingly finance proven capability.

Company financing and infrastructure financing are distinct problems. The operating company does not necessarily need to own every physical asset it coordinates.

Capital must not silently rewrite the architecture, compromise vendor neutrality, weaken security, grant inappropriate production-control authority, or override legitimate governance.

## 7. Capital Allocation

Available capital should be conceptually separated into:

- **Survival capital** — protects organizational continuity and existing obligations;
- **Execution capital** — funds the current roadmap/evidence gate;
- **Future capital** — funds bounded research and preparation for later stages;
- **Contingency capital** — protects against unexpected operational and financial events.

Capital follows strategy, and strategy follows evidence.

The current roadmap gate receives priority over speculative future-stage expansion. Future National DERMS ambitions should not starve the Facility EMS evidence required to justify them.

Material internal investment requests should answer:

1. What problem is being solved?
2. Why now?
3. Which roadmap gate does it support?
4. How much capital is required?
5. What capability or evidence will result?
6. What alternatives exist?
7. What happens if the expenditure is not made?
8. Is the expenditure reversible?
9. Who owns the outcome?
10. How will success be measured?

Budgets are permission to pursue outcomes, not obligations to spend every allocated shilling. New evidence may increase, redirect, or terminate an allocation.

## 8. Financial Controls

Money should move through an auditable control chain:

```text
Need
 ↓
Budget
 ↓
Request
 ↓
Approval
 ↓
Procurement / Contract
 ↓
Delivery Verification
 ↓
Invoice Verification
 ↓
Payment Authorization
 ↓
Payment
 ↓
Accounting
 ↓
Reconciliation
 ↓
Reporting / Audit
```

Financial control begins when an obligation is created, not merely when cash leaves the bank.

Key principles include:

- segregation of duties where practical;
- approval authority proportional to consequence;
- documented procurement decisions;
- supplier identity and payment-detail verification;
- purchase-order, receipt, and invoice matching for material purchases;
- project accounting;
- restricted-fund tracking;
- expense evidence and business purpose;
- controlled bank and payment authority;
- regular reconciliation;
- asset registers;
- defined contract-signing authority;
- conflict-of-interest disclosure;
- reconstructable audit trails.

No individual's status, including founder status, grants unlimited financial authority.

## 9. Financial Planning and Scenario Model

The planning model connects operational reality to financial outcomes.

```text
Deployment Plan
      ↓
Installed Facilities
      ↓
Revenue Model
      ↓
Cost Model
      ↓
People / Capacity Model
      ↓
Cash Flow
      ↓
Capital Requirement
```

### Core relationships

```text
Opening Facilities
+ New Deployments
- Churned Facilities
= Closing Facilities
```

```text
New Facilities
× Average Deployment Revenue
= Deployment Revenue
```

```text
Active Managed Facilities
× Average Managed-Service Revenue
= Recurring Revenue
```

The model should connect deployment demand to engineering and delivery capacity and connect the installed base to operational capacity.

Forecasts should include at minimum:

- downside scenario;
- base scenario;
- upside scenario.

Stress tests should examine payment delays, hardware inflation, slower deployment, higher support burden, customer loss, foreign-exchange movements, staffing requirements, and delayed strategic projects.

Future-stage revenue should not be used to make today's economics appear viable before the relevant evidence gate has been passed.

Actual results continuously update assumptions.

## 11. Risk, Insurance and Financial Resilience

Material risks should be handled through a layered process:

```text
Identify
  ↓
Prevent
  ↓
Reduce / Contain
  ↓
Contractually Allocate
  ↓
Transfer where appropriate
  ↓
Absorb residual risk
```

Risk domains include operational, technology, cybersecurity, customer credit, contractual, product liability, people, supply chain, currency, regulatory, project, concentration, and catastrophic external risks.

The resilience architecture may include:

```text
Engineering + Security + Safety
          ↓
Contractual Allocation
          ↓
Operating / Maintenance Reserves
          ↓
Appropriate Insurance
          ↓
Corporate Liquidity / Stress Capital
```

Insurance never replaces engineering, cybersecurity, safety, or operational discipline.

Major projects should be evaluated on expected return and downside exposure. No individual project should create a reasonably foreseeable loss capable of unnecessarily destroying the institution.

The exact insurance programme, liability wording, and legal allocation of risk must be developed with qualified professional advisers as deployment responsibility and jurisdictional exposure mature.

## 12. Profit, Surplus and Reinvestment

Profit is not the mission. It is a mechanism for sustaining responsibility, building independence, and financing future capability.

Conceptual surplus waterfall:

```text
Revenue
  ↓
Direct Costs
  ↓
Gross Contribution
  ↓
Operating Expenses
  ↓
Operating Surplus
  ↓
Taxes / Financing Obligations
  ↓
Net Surplus
  ↓
Capital Allocation
```

The preferred allocation hierarchy is:

1. meet existing obligations;
2. protect infrastructure responsibility already accepted;
3. maintain adequate reserves and liquidity;
4. fund the current roadmap gate;
5. reinvest in high-leverage reusable capability;
6. reduce expensive or unnecessary financial risk;
7. fund bounded future-stage research;
8. strengthen the open-source and delivery ecosystem where strategically justified;
9. return genuinely excess capital to owners when appropriate.

Founder/executive compensation for work and shareholder returns on ownership are economically distinct and should remain separately governed.

Retained earnings create strategic independence by increasing the organization's ability to reject capital, contracts, or partnerships that compromise its architecture or mission.

## 13. Financial Governance

Financial governance separates stewardship from day-to-day financial management.

```text
Governance
   ↓
Executive Leadership
   ↓
Finance
   ↓
Budget Owners / Teams
   ↓
Execution
   ↓
Reporting + Audit
   ↺
```

### Governance responsibilities

Governance should oversee, at a level appropriate to organizational maturity:

- financial plans and budgets;
- major capital allocation;
- financing strategy;
- material debt;
- equity issuance;
- reserves;
- major investments;
- solvency and liquidity;
- related-party transactions;
- financial risk;
- audit and financial reporting;
- transformational transactions.

### Executive responsibilities

Executive leadership translates strategy into budgets, hiring, investment, growth rate, liquidity requirements, and portfolio allocation within governance-approved boundaries.

### Finance responsibilities

Finance is an independent stewardship function responsible for accounting, treasury, cash forecasting, budgeting, controls, reporting, receivables, payables, financial risk, and audit support.

Finance must be able to reject commitments that lack financial authorization even when they are commercially or technically attractive.

### Delegation of authority

A formal Delegation of Financial Authority should eventually define approval thresholds based on amount, risk, duration, and consequence. Exact KES thresholds should be established from actual capitalization and operating scale rather than invented prematurely.

Material equity issuance, debt, major asset pledges, transformational transactions, and other reserved financial matters require governance authority appropriate to their consequences.

Emergency authority may accelerate action but does not remove post-event accountability.

## 14. Financial Digital Twin

The organization should ultimately maintain a continuously improving model of its own economics.

```text
Real Organization
      │
      │ operational + financial data
      ▼
Financial Model
      │
      │ scenarios + forecasts
      ▼
Decisions
      │
      ▼
Real Organization
```

Accounting records what happened.

The financial model estimates what may happen under explicit assumptions.

Actual results are compared with forecasts, variances are investigated, assumptions are updated, and the model becomes more accurate as the organization gains evidence.

This creates a financial analogue of a digital twin: a decision system for understanding organizational capacity, cash requirements, economic limits, and scale.

## 15. Financial Laws

The Financial Model is governed by the following laws:

1. **Responsibility must have an economic model.**
2. **Deployment economics and recurring economics are measured separately.**
3. **Every facility must eventually have a measurable cost to serve.**
4. **Custom engineering should progressively become reusable platform capability.**
5. **Profitability does not replace liquidity.**
6. **Customer-specific expenditure should not silently turn the organization into the customer's bank.**
7. **Match capital to the risk, duration, and activity it finances.**
8. **Company financing and infrastructure financing are distinct problems.**
9. **Capital follows strategy, and strategy follows evidence.**
10. **The current evidence gate takes priority over speculative future-stage expansion.**
11. **Financial authority is proportional to responsibility and consequence, not status.**
12. **Material transactions must be reconstructable and independently reviewable.**
13. **Forecasts are models of reality, not promises about reality.**
14. **Future-stage revenue does not finance today's story before its evidence gate is passed.**
15. **Cash, receivables, concentration, and working-capital exposure remain visible regardless of reported profitability.**
16. **Risk should be prevented and contained before residual financial exposure is transferred.**
17. **No project should unnecessarily threaten institutional continuity.**
18. **Existing infrastructure responsibility is funded before discretionary expansion or distribution.**
19. **Economic success should increase strategic independence and mission capacity.**
20. **Organizational capital is governed in stewardship for the mission.**

## 16. Stage Evolution

The financial architecture matures with infrastructure responsibility.

### Laboratory / Research

Focus on burn, runway, prototype cost, grants, founder/internal capital, basic accounting, and evidence generation.

### Facility Pilot

Add deployment economics, project cash flow, engineering effort, commissioning cost, strategic-pilot classification, and customer/payment evidence.

### Commercial Facility EMS

Add recurring revenue, cost to serve, margins, CAC, retention, expansion, working-capital controls, project accounting, and operational reserves.

### Regional DERMS

Add portfolio economics, partner economics, regional operating costs, formal treasury, stronger financial governance, risk management, and appropriate development/project finance.

### Utility / National Infrastructure

Add institutional infrastructure finance, advanced risk governance, formal audit, public/DFI accountability, infrastructure-level resilience, project structures, and governance proportional to national-scale responsibility.

> **Governance, controls, financing, and reporting complexity grow with consequence.**

---

## 17. Final Doctrine

The Financial Model exists to make the mission economically durable.

The organization should not pursue revenue at the expense of reliability, growth at the expense of liquidity, capital at the expense of independence, or national scale before evidence demonstrates readiness.

The desired financial institution is one that can:

- finance learning;
- convert learning into reusable capability;
- deploy that capability economically;
- earn recurring revenue from clearly defined responsibility;
- survive shocks;
- protect existing infrastructure obligations;
- fund the next evidence gate;
- attract increasingly appropriate capital as uncertainty falls;
- preserve open interoperability and institutional independence;
- and continuously reinvest economic strength into greater energy-infrastructure coordination capability.

> **Master Financial Law:** Every financial decision should strengthen, or deliberately and measurably advance, the organization's ability to coordinate energy infrastructure responsibly over the long term.
