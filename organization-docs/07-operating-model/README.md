# Operating Model

This section defines how the organization executes its mission in practice: how problems become work, authority is distributed, systems are deployed and operated, knowledge is retained, partners participate, and the institution is governed.

## Main Document

- [Operating Model](./operating-model.md)

## Core Operating Thesis

> The organization operates as a distributed problem-solving system: responsibility and execution are placed as close as practical to the people and systems possessing the relevant knowledge, while shared standards, evidence, architecture, and governance preserve coordination toward one mission.

## Execution Loop

```text
Problem → Understand → Build → Prove → Operate → Learn → Repeat
```

## Operating Model Structure

The model is organized into thirteen connected systems:

1. **Execution Engine** — converts real-world problems into validated capability and learning.
2. **Organizational Structure** — creates structure around persistent responsibility.
3. **Roles, Ownership & Accountability** — assigns one identifiable owner to important outcomes.
4. **Decision-Making System** — places authority at the lowest competent and sufficiently authorized level.
5. **Work Management System** — converts requests into understood, prioritized, owned, validated work.
6. **Operating Cadence** — coordinates real-time, daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, gate, and annual decision horizons.
7. **Knowledge & Documentation System** — converts individual learning into organizational memory.
8. **Quality, Safety & Release Management** — requires evidence proportional to consequence before production control authority is granted.
9. **Incident & Reliability Management** — contains failures, preserves essential service, restores capability, and converts incidents into learning.
10. **Security Operating Model** — protects people, infrastructure, information, and control authority through bounded trust.
11. **Customer & Deployment Operating Model** — qualifies, deploys, commissions, hands over, operates, proves, and expands customer systems.
12. **Partner & Open-Source Operating Model** — distributes execution while preserving interoperability through standards, evidence, and governance.
13. **Governance Model** — separates mission, corporate, executive, technical, open-source, operational, and security authority.

## Governing Principle

> **Distribute execution. Preserve local autonomy. Standardize interfaces. Demand evidence. Coordinate at the appropriate level. Protect the mission through governance.**

## Organizational–Technical Alignment

The organization deliberately follows the same architectural logic as the energy system it is building:

```text
ENERGY SYSTEM
Distributed resources
+ Local autonomy
+ Shared standards
+ Higher-level coordination

ORGANIZATION
Distributed people and partners
+ Local authority
+ Shared principles and standards
+ Higher-level coordination
```

The organization therefore does not merely build distributed coordination infrastructure. **It operates according to the same principles.**
