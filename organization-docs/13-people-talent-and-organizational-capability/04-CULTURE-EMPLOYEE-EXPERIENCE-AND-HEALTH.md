# Culture, Employee Experience & Organizational Health

## Purpose

Define the behavioral environment in which the institution operates.

> Culture is the collection of behaviors an organization repeatedly rewards, tolerates, corrects and teaches.

```text
Values → Behaviors → Working Norms → Daily Decisions → Culture → Organizational Health
```

## Core Principles

- Solve real problems.
- Own outcomes.
- Evidence over assumption.
- Open standards.
- Document what matters.
- Raise risks early.
- Safety before optimization.
- Learn through building.
- Collaborate without losing accountability.
- Leave systems better than you found them.

## Communication

Use communication channels according to purpose:

- Urgent issue → synchronous escalation
- Complex decision → written proposal and appropriate discussion
- Routine update → asynchronous communication
- Collaborative problem → working session
- Permanent knowledge → documentation

Important decisions should remain discoverable.

## Psychological Safety & Accountability

People must be able to say:

- I do not know.
- I made a mistake.
- I disagree.
- I need help.
- This looks unsafe.
- Our assumption may be wrong.

Psychological safety does not remove performance standards or accountability.

## Safety Culture

```text
Safety > Reliability > Correctness > Optimization
```

Legitimate safety concerns must have escalation paths regardless of hierarchy or schedule pressure.

## Sustainable Performance

When demand exceeds capacity, prioritize, de-scope, automate, delay or add capability. Permanent overwork is not a sustainable operating model.

Burnout, repeated emergencies and excessive on-call burden should be treated as organizational signals requiring system improvement.

## Knowledge Culture

Reject knowledge hoarding. Reward documentation, teaching, automation, mentoring and succession.

## Organizational Health

Monitor signals such as voluntary and regrettable attrition, workload, manager effectiveness, time to productivity, internal mobility, engagement, succession coverage, on-call burden and psychological safety.

## Culture Anti-Patterns

Avoid founder worship, hero culture, fear of bad news, knowledge silos, permanent crunch, blame culture, title-based truth, metric gaming, political information silos and silent disagreement.

## Definition of Done

Culture is mature when principles have observable behavioral meaning; leaders follow the same standards; people can challenge responsibly; safety escalation is protected; accountability remains clear; important knowledge is discoverable; workload is sustainable; feedback produces learning; and the organization periodically refactors its own processes and cultural debt.
