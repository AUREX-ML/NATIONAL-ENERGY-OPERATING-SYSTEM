# Organizational Design & Workforce Architecture

## Purpose

Define how strategy becomes organizational capability, functions, roles, authority, teams and workforce demand.

## Design Chain

```text
Strategy → Work → Capabilities → Functions → Roles → People → Teams → Outcomes
```

## Principles

1. Design the organization around work and capabilities rather than personalities.
2. Every critical outcome has a clear owner.
3. Responsibility must be accompanied by sufficient authority.
4. Keep spans, layers and coordination mechanisms appropriate to organizational scale.
5. Distinguish positions from the people who occupy them.
6. Distinguish capability from capacity.
7. Use employees, contractors, partners, automation and open-source contributors according to the nature of the work.
8. Keep mission-critical ownership inside the accountable institution even when external contributors participate.

## Capability Map

The energy-infrastructure organization should progressively develop capability across energy systems, software/platform engineering, edge/IoT, cybersecurity, data/AI, field deployment, operations, customer/service delivery, finance, commercial development, legal/regulatory, governance and organizational leadership.

## Role Architecture

Every material role should define:

- Purpose and expected outcomes
- Responsibilities
- Decision rights
- Required capabilities
- Reporting relationship
- Interfaces with other functions
- Performance evidence
- Criticality and succession requirements

## Workforce Planning

```text
Strategic objective
      ↓
Work required
      ↓
Capability required
      ↓
Existing coverage
      ↓
Capability/capacity gap
      ↓
Build / Hire / Contract / Partner / Automate
```

Plan across immediate, 6–12 month, and 12–24+ month horizons.

## Organizational Scaling

### Founder-led prototype
Keep structure minimal. Document responsibilities, critical knowledge and external specialists.

### Pilot organization
Introduce clear functional ownership, workforce planning, formal employment/contractor arrangements and repeatable onboarding.

### Commercial organization
Introduce management layers only where coordination demands them, alongside job levels, specialized teams and workforce analytics.

### Infrastructure institution
Develop regional operating structures, leadership pipelines, deep succession and formal governance of critical operational roles.

## Definition of Done

Organizational design is mature when critical capabilities are known; every critical outcome has ownership; authority is explicit; workforce gaps are visible; headcount follows strategy and economics; critical roles have redundancy; and structure can scale without depending on informal founder knowledge.
