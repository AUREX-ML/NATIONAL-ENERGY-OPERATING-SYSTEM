# Business Strategy

This section defines how the organization converts its energy-coordination mission into a financially sustainable, scalable, and institutionally resilient business.

## Main Document

- [Business Strategy](./business-strategy.md)

## Core Business Thesis

> The organization transforms fragmented energy infrastructure into coordinated energy systems and earns revenue by accepting defined, measurable responsibility for keeping that coordination infrastructure reliable, secure, and useful.

## Scope

The Business Strategy defines the organization's approach to:

- managed energy-infrastructure coordination as the core business;
- customer, buyer, payer, user, and beneficiary architecture;
- value creation from reducing energy-system fragmentation;
- deployment and recurring managed-service revenue;
- evidence-led go-to-market expansion;
- competitive advantage through interoperability, operating evidence, ecosystem scale, and institutional trust;
- Build–Collaborate–Partner decisions;
- funding matched to technical, commercial, and infrastructure risk;
- disciplined unit, project, and working-capital economics;
- Energy Infrastructure Under Trusted Coordination as the North Star;
- organizational resilience through distributed dependency and coordinated governance.

## Governing Principles

1. Revenue follows operational responsibility.
2. Recurring revenue must be earned through recurring value, not artificial proprietary dependence.
3. Sell to the institution that owns the coordination problem.
4. Land → Solve → Prove → Reference → Expand → Aggregate → Move Up.
5. Build competitive advantage without trapping customers.
6. Build what defines coordination capability; collaborate where openness creates strength; partner outside the core.
7. Match capital to risk.
8. Customer-specific deployment costs should not silently consume core operating capital.
9. Measure coordination achieved, value delivered, responsibility sustained, and trust earned.
10. Distribute unnecessary dependency while preserving coordinated governance.

## Relationship to the Organization Framework

The **Product Portfolio** defines what the organization builds.
