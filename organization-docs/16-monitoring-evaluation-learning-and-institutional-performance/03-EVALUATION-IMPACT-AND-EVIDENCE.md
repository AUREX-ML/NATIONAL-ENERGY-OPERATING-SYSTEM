# Evaluation, Impact & Evidence

## Purpose

Evaluation determines whether interventions produced meaningful outcomes, why observed change occurred, whether the organization contributed to it, and whether results justify continuation or scale.

## Evaluation Chain

```text
Intervention
    ↓
Evaluation Question
    ↓
Method
    ↓
Baseline / Comparison
    ↓
Evidence
    ↓
Finding
    ↓
Confidence / Limitations
    ↓
Decision
```

## Evaluation Questions

Before consequential pilots or interventions, define what must be learned and what evidence would constitute success, failure or an inconclusive result.

## Outputs, Outcomes and Impact

```text
Output  = what was delivered
Outcome = what changed for users or operations
Impact  = broader consequential change
```

These shall not be conflated.

## Causality

Observed change does not automatically prove causation. Evaluation shall distinguish:

- Observation
- Correlation
- Contribution
- Attribution

Claims should remain within the capability of the evaluation method.

## Implementation Fidelity

When an intervention fails, determine whether the theory failed or whether implementation materially differed from the intended intervention.

## Evidence Classes

A useful institutional classification is:

```text
E0 — Assertion
E1 — Anecdotal
E2 — Documented
E3 — System-measured
E4 — Comparative / causal
E5 — Independently verified
```

Required strength rises with the consequence of the claim or decision.

## Evidence Register

Consequential evidence should record:

- Evidence ID
- Claim or question
- Source
- Method
- Date
- Owner
- Strength
- Limitations
- Verification state
- Status

## Claim Governance

Material external claims such as energy savings, emissions reduction, jobs created, downtime reduction, public money saved or grid-reliability improvement require explicit evidence review before publication.

```text
Claim → Evidence → Quality Review → Approval → Publication
```

## Evaluation Independence

Independence should rise with consequence:

```text
Self-assessment
      ↓
Cross-function review
      ↓
Internal assurance
      ↓
External review
      ↓
Independent verification
```

## Economic Evaluation

Economic evaluation should consider lifecycle effects rather than only acquisition cost. Relevant measures may include deployment cost, operating cost, avoided losses, maintenance, asset life, customer value and scalability economics.

## Sustainability and Scalability

A technically successful intervention is not automatically sustainable or scalable. Evaluate technical, operational, commercial, institutional and ecosystem readiness separately.

## Principles

1. Evaluation questions precede evaluation results.
2. Pilot success criteria are defined before pilots begin.
3. Observed change is not automatically attributed to the intervention.
4. Evaluation rigor rises with consequence.
5. Claims never exceed evidence strength.
6. Negative and inconclusive findings are preserved.
7. Material uncertainty and limitations are reported.
8. Independent assurance increases with public consequence.

## Definition of Done

Evaluation is mature when consequential interventions have predefined questions and success criteria, methods are appropriate to the claims being made, implementation fidelity is understood, causal language is disciplined, evidence strength and uncertainty are explicit, negative findings remain accessible, and scaling or public-impact claims can withstand appropriate assurance.
