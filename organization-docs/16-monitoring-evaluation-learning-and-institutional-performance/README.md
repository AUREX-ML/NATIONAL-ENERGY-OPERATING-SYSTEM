# 16 — Monitoring, Evaluation, Learning & Institutional Performance

This module defines how the organization measures whether its mission, products, operations and infrastructure are actually producing the intended outcomes; how trustworthy evidence is generated; how interventions are evaluated; and how learning changes decisions, standards and strategy.

> **Master MEL Law:** The organization shall maintain an integrated monitoring, evaluation and learning system that connects mission and strategy to measurable outcomes; converts physical, digital, operational, commercial and institutional reality into governed evidence; preserves authoritative definitions, baselines, targets, lineage, quality, ownership and uncertainty throughout the measurement chain; evaluates consequential interventions using methods proportionate to their risk and claims; distinguishes activity from outcome, observation from causation, and contribution from attribution; prevents unsupported narratives, vanity metrics and metric gaming from substituting for demonstrated performance; converts evidence, incidents, experiments and external change into explicit learning and accountable decisions; verifies whether resulting adaptations improve the system; and requires increasingly strong evidence before the institution assumes increasingly consequential responsibility for Africa's distributed energy infrastructure.

## Definition of Done

The domain is mature when mission and strategy connect to measurable outcomes, consequential metrics have governed definitions and authoritative sources, monitoring is reliable, evaluations are designed before claims are made, evidence quality and uncertainty are explicit, lessons produce durable changes, consequential decisions retain rationale and evidence, and scale transitions are governed by increasingly strong proof rather than ambition alone.
