# Integrated MEL Governance & Control Framework

## Purpose

This framework integrates performance measurement, monitoring, evaluation, evidence, learning and decision-making into one institutional control system.

## Integrated Architecture

```text
Mission
  ↓
Theory of Change
  ↓
Strategic Objectives
  ↓
Performance Framework
  ↓
Monitoring
  ↓
Evaluation
  ↓
Evidence
  ↓
Learning
  ↓
Decision
  ↓
Adaptation
  ↓
New Performance
  └──────────────→ loop
```

## Governance Layers

```text
Board / Governing Body
          ↓
Executive Leadership
          ↓
Domain / Function Owners
          ↓
Operating Teams
```

The governing body oversees mission performance, systemic risk, financial sustainability, material impact claims and MEL integrity. Executive leadership owns strategic KPIs, resource allocation, major evaluations, scale gates and strategic adaptation. Domain owners remain accountable for evidence generated by their areas.

## Separation of Responsibilities

Where consequence justifies it:

```text
Project execution ≠ sole evaluation
Data production ≠ sole data assurance
Public claim author ≠ sole claim approver
```

## Core Registers

The integrated system maintains:

- Metric Dictionary
- Baseline Register

- Evaluation Register
- Hypothesis Register

- Decision Register
- Corrective Action Register
- Claim Register

## Reporting Architecture

```text
Real-time Operations
Weekly Delivery
Monthly Performance
Quarterly Strategy
Annual Impact
Event-driven Exception Reporting
```

Exception reporting overrides normal cadence for critical KPI breaches, major incidents, material data failures, public-claim errors, significant financial variance or evidence that invalidates important strategy assumptions.

## Performance Assurance

Periodically verify metric definitions, source systems, calculations, data quality, dashboard accuracy, claims and evaluation methods. Independence should increase with consequence.

## Integrity Controls

Protect against:

- Unauthorized metric-definition changes
- Manual overrides without records
- Selective exclusion
- Double counting
- Backdated target changes
- Unsupported causal language
- Cherry-picked results
- Metric gaming

## Counter-Metrics

Important optimization metrics should be paired with measures that expose system damage. For example, deployment speed should be reviewed alongside defect rate, support load, security and customer value.

## Scale-Gate Governance

```text
Facility EMS
     ↓ evidence gate
Regional DERMS
     ↓ evidence gate
National DERMS
```

### Facility Gate

Demonstrate interoperability, reliability, customer value, repeatable deployment, security and viable economics.

### Regional Gate

Demonstrate multi-facility coordination, regional reliability, scalable operations, governance, resilience, utility value and scalable economics.

### National Gate

Require legitimate authority, proven infrastructure, cyber resilience, continuity, institutional capability, economic sustainability, ecosystem readiness and public-interest governance.

> **Evidence Gate Law:** Scale shall follow evidence rather than ambition.

## MEL Maturity Model

```text
Level 0 — Unknown
No consistent measurement

Level 1 — Reactive
Data collected after problems

Level 2 — Documented
Metrics and reports defined

Level 3 — Managed
Targets, reviews and ownership

Level 4 — Evidence-Driven
Evaluation, decisions and learning

Level 5 — Adaptive
Continuous evidence drives organizational adaptation
```

Assess maturity separately across measurement, data, monitoring, evaluation, impact, learning, decision-making, assurance and reporting.

## Implementation Sequence

```text
1. Theory of Change
2. Critical Metrics
3. Data Sources
4. Monitoring
5. Performance Reviews
6. Evaluation
7. Learning System
8. Impact + Assurance
```

Early prototypes should prioritize technical reliability, data quality, interoperability, facility value, deployment effort and critical assumptions rather than creating unnecessary reporting bureaucracy.

## Failure Condition

The framework is failing when dashboards exist but decisions ignore them, reports use conflicting definitions, pilots lack predefined success criteria, claims cannot be traced to evidence, lessons exist but failures repeat, or data accumulates without clear decision use.

## Success Condition

## Integrated MEL Laws

1. Measurement exists to improve decisions, not maximize reporting.
2. Mission and strategy determine what matters enough to measure.
3. Every consequential metric has one governed definition and accountable owner.
4. Baselines precede credible improvement claims.
5. Outputs, outcomes and impacts are never interchangeable.
6. Observed change is not automatically attributed to the organization.
7. Evaluation rigor rises with consequence.
8. Claims never exceed evidence strength.
9. Consequential evidence remains traceable to source and methodology.
10. Negative findings are preserved.
11. Material uncertainty is communicated.
12. Evidence informs authority but does not replace legitimate decision rights.
13. Significant performance variance produces an explicit response.
14. Lessons must produce owned changes before institutionalization.
15. Successful patterns are validated before standards are created.
16. Critical assumptions remain open to disconfirmation.
17. Resource allocation changes when strong evidence requires it.
18. Scale is an evidence-gated privilege.

## Master MEL Law

> The organization shall maintain an integrated monitoring, evaluation and learning system that connects mission and strategy to measurable outcomes; converts physical, digital, operational, commercial and institutional reality into governed evidence; preserves authoritative definitions, baselines, targets, lineage, quality, ownership and uncertainty throughout the measurement chain; evaluates consequential interventions using methods proportionate to their risk and claims; distinguishes activity from outcome, observation from causation, and contribution from attribution; prevents unsupported narratives, vanity metrics and metric gaming from substituting for demonstrated performance; converts evidence, incidents, experiments and external change into explicit learning and accountable decisions; verifies whether resulting adaptations improve the system; and requires increasingly strong evidence before the institution assumes increasingly consequential responsibility for Africa's distributed energy infrastructure.

## Definition of Done

Domain 16 is complete when the institution can connect mission to measurable outcomes; maintain governed indicators, baselines, targets and authoritative sources; monitor quality and lineage; evaluate consequential interventions; distinguish observation, contribution and attribution; govern impact claims; preserve negative evidence and uncertainty; test critical assumptions; record consequential decisions; convert lessons into verified institutional changes; transfer knowledge across personnel and facilities; adapt strategy when evidence changes; and require increasingly strong proof for increasingly consequential scale transitions.
