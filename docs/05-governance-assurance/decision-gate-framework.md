## 1. Purpose

This document defines the decision gates that should govern progression of the National Energy Operating System Programme from concept to pilot, from pilot to productization, and from local proof toward regional and national readiness.

The framework exists to ensure that programme scale follows evidence, authority, and readiness rather than ambition alone.

## 2. Gate Principle

Each gate asks one disciplined question:

> Is there enough evidence and legitimate authority to accept the next level of responsibility?

A gate is not a meeting for general updates. It is a decision point with inputs, evidence, accountable reviewers, possible outcomes, and recorded rationale.

## 3. Gate Outcomes

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Proceed | Evidence and authority are sufficient for the next step. |
| Proceed with conditions | The next step may begin after specific conditions are met. |
| Hold | The programme remains at the current stage until gaps are resolved. |
| Redesign | The proposed path is not acceptable and must be changed. |
| Stop | The programme should not continue in the current form. |

## 5. Gate 0 — Problem Validation

### Decision Question

Is the distributed energy coordination problem real, material, and validated enough to justify a structured discovery and pilot-readiness phase?

### Required Inputs

- problem statement;
- sector context;
- stakeholder review;
- initial regulatory and institutional questions;
- known assumptions and uncertainties.

### Evidence Required

- stakeholder feedback;
- corrected assumptions;
- confirmed problem dimensions;
- unresolved validation questions;
- recommendation for discovery scope.

### Possible Outcomes

- proceed to pilot readiness;
- refine problem statement;
- narrow programme scope;
- pause until stronger evidence exists.

## 6. Gate 1 — Pilot Readiness

### Decision Question

Is there a suitable Facility EMS pilot site, authority, baseline, risk control, and implementation plan?

### Required Inputs

- pilot readiness checklist;
- candidate site assessment;
- stakeholder and authority map;
- technical assessment;
- safety and cybersecurity review;
- data-governance plan;
- budget and resourcing plan;
- acceptance criteria.

### Evidence Required

- named site decision owner;
- approved pilot scope;
- baseline plan;
- minimum controls;
- legal and regulatory screening;
- implementation plan.

### Possible Outcomes

- proceed to pilot implementation;
- proceed only after conditions;
- select a different site;
- redesign pilot;
- hold.

## 7. Gate 2 — Facility EMS Evidence

### Decision Question

Did the Facility EMS pilot prove useful, safe, reliable, measurable, and repeatable enough to justify a second pilot or productization phase?

### Required Inputs

- final pilot evidence report;
- technical performance data;
- operator feedback;
- safety and incident review;
- cybersecurity findings;
- economic and operational value assessment;
- productization backlog.

### Evidence Required

- reliable telemetry and system state;
- documented operator use;
- measured value against baseline;
- safe failure behaviour;
- controlled access and audit;
- lessons learned;
- second-site or productization recommendation.

### Possible Outcomes

- proceed to second facility pilot;
- productize selected capability;
- extend current pilot;
- redesign technical architecture;
- stop facility approach.

## 8. Gate 3 — Product Repeatability

### Decision Question

Can Facility EMS be deployed, operated, supported, and improved repeatedly without rebuilding the solution for every site?

### Required Inputs

- deployment playbook;
- integration catalogue;
- configuration model;
- support model;
- unit economics;
- security model;
- documentation and training;
- operational runbooks.

### Evidence Required

- reduced site-specific engineering effort;
- standard commissioning process;
- support and maintenance records;
- repeatable economics;
- defined product boundaries;
- customer acceptance.

### Possible Outcomes

- proceed to repeatable product phase;
- run additional pilots;
- narrow product scope;
- redesign delivery model;
- hold until repeatability improves.

## 9. Gate 4 — Portfolio Aggregation

### Decision Question

Can multiple facilities be represented as one portfolio while preserving facility autonomy, permissions, and local safe operation?

### Required Inputs

- multi-site architecture;
- identity and access model;
- facility capability abstraction;
- ownership hierarchy;
- data and consent model;
- aggregation logic;
- failure-mode analysis.

### Evidence Required

- portfolio visibility;
- facility-level drill-down;
- standardized facility capability;
- clear participation permissions;
- continued local operation during upstream failure;
- operator acceptance.

### Possible Outcomes

- proceed to portfolio capability;
- continue single-facility productization;
- redesign aggregation model;
- hold.

## 10. Gate 5 — Regional DERMS Readiness

### Decision Question

Is regional distributed-energy coordination technically, legally, operationally, and institutionally feasible in a bounded environment?

### Required Inputs

- regional use case;
- participating facilities or assets;
- regional operator or sponsor;
- authority and consent model;
- technical architecture;
- network constraints or regional objective;
- measurement plan;
- risk and assurance plan.

### Evidence Required

- authorized regional objective;
- facility capability aggregation;
- dispatch or coordination request model;
- verification method;
- failure containment;
- stakeholder acceptance;
- legal and regulatory screening.

### Possible Outcomes

- proceed to regional prototype;
- run additional facility/portfolio pilots;
- narrow regional scope;
- pause pending mandate or technical readiness.

## 11. Gate 6 — National Architecture Readiness

### Decision Question

Is there sufficient evidence, mandate, institutional design, technical maturity, funding logic, and public-interest justification to develop a national programme design?

### Required Inputs

- facility and regional evidence;
- validated stakeholder and authority map;
- regulatory requirements register;
- national architecture;
- cybersecurity and resilience model;
- procurement and funding options;
- public-value case;
- implementation and assurance plan.

### Evidence Required

- proven lower-level coordination;
- confirmed institutional roles;
- credible cost-benefit case;
- national data and interoperability model;
- operational readiness plan;
- cybersecurity requirements;
- governance and accountability model;
- funding and sustainability model.

### Possible Outcomes

- proceed to national programme design;
- continue regional validation;
- narrow national scope to research or standards;
- hold pending official mandate;
- stop national path.

## 12. Gate Record Template

Each gate decision should record:

- gate name;
- date;
- decision owner;
- reviewers;
- evidence reviewed;
- decision outcome;
- conditions or actions;
- risks accepted;
- unresolved issues;
- next review trigger.

## 13. Current Gate Position

The programme is currently before **Gate 0 — Problem Validation** and preparing materials needed for **Gate 1 — Pilot Readiness**.

The immediate objective is not national deployment. The immediate objective is validated discovery and a credible Facility EMS pilot pathway.
