# Programme Definition

This section defines what the initiative is, why it exists, what problem it addresses, and what must be validated before official programme decisions are made.

## Core Definition

The National Energy Operating System Programme is a proposed public-interest digital infrastructure initiative for coordinating distributed energy resources across facilities, regional systems, and national energy-sector functions.

It is not merely a software application. It is a programme that combines:

- energy-system architecture;
- distributed energy resource coordination;
- facility and regional operating models;
- data and interoperability standards;
- cybersecurity and resilience;
- institutional governance;
- regulatory alignment;
- market and settlement evidence;
- implementation and assurance discipline.

## Recommended Use

The Executive Brief is the first document for visitors and senior stakeholders.

The Programme Charter defines scope, governance, workstreams, decision gates, deliverables, and immediate next steps.

The Stakeholder and Institutional Map clarifies who may be affected, who may contribute, and which authority questions require validation.

The Problem Statement and Needs Assessment provides the longer technical and policy justification.

## Current Programme Stage

The programme is at **working architecture and validation-preparation stage**.

The material is strong enough to support structured discussion with government, utilities, regulators, technical partners, and development institutions. It is not yet evidence that a national system has been deployed, approved, procured, or operationalized.

## Validation Priorities

- Confirm the national problem statement with responsible institutions.
- Map legal and regulatory authority boundaries.
- Identify potential facility and regional pilot environments.
- Establish baseline studies and evidence requirements.
- Determine whether a national programme mandate, public-private structure, or existing institutional owner is appropriate.
