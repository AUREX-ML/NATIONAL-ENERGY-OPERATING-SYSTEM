## 1. Purpose

This document maps the stakeholder and institutional landscape for the proposed National Energy Operating System Programme.

Its purpose is to clarify who may be affected, who may contribute, who may authorize, who may operate, who may regulate, who may benefit, and which questions require formal validation before any pilot or national-scale decision.

This map is not a legal mandate, procurement structure, or final governance model. It is a working architecture for stakeholder review.

## 2. Institutional Principle

The programme must distinguish between:

- stakeholders who are affected;
- stakeholders who contribute knowledge or resources;
- stakeholders who own assets;
- stakeholders who operate systems;
- stakeholders who regulate or approve;
- stakeholders who procure or fund;
- stakeholders who may use programme outputs;
- stakeholders who have formal decision authority.

Interest does not automatically create authority. Authority must come from law, regulation, licence, contract, delegation, ownership, or approved governance.

## 3. Stakeholder Landscape

```text
Government and Policy Institutions
        ↓
Regulator and Statutory Authorities
        ↓
System, Transmission, Distribution and Market Functions
        ↓
Facilities, Asset Owners and Energy Users
        ↓
Technology, Research, Finance and Delivery Ecosystem
        ↓
Consumers, Communities and Public Interest
```

The National Energy Operating System Programme sits between these groups as a proposed digital coordination and evidence layer. It must support legitimate institutional roles rather than replace them.

## 4. Stakeholder Groups

### 4.1 National Government and Policy Institutions

Potential roles:

- define national energy-policy priorities;
- determine whether the programme aligns with public-interest objectives;
- sponsor discovery, coordination, or policy review where appropriate;
- convene relevant public institutions;
- guide public infrastructure, digital transformation, industrialization, climate, and energy-transition alignment.

Validation required:

- Which ministry, department, agency, or inter-institutional mechanism should host or sponsor early-stage review?
- Which policy objectives should the programme explicitly support?
- What official process is required before pilot endorsement or public-sector participation?

### 4.2 Energy Regulator and Statutory Authorities

Potential roles:

- interpret applicable energy regulations;
- define licensing, approval, reporting, consumer-protection, and compliance requirements;
- determine conditions for aggregation, dispatch, market participation, open access, or settlement-related functions;
- validate regulatory treatment of data, metering, and operational records.

Validation required:

- Which proposed functions require regulatory approval?
- Which activities are informational, advisory, operational, commercial, or regulated?
- What evidence is acceptable for compliance, audit, and settlement support?

### 4.3 Transmission, Distribution and System Operation Functions

Potential roles:

- define network and system-operation requirements;
- provide operational constraints, interconnection requirements, grid-code expectations, and technical acceptance criteria;
- participate in pilots where distributed energy coordination affects network operations;
- validate telemetry, forecasting, control, flexibility, congestion, curtailment, and operational integration needs.

Validation required:

- Which interfaces are needed with existing control, planning, metering, outage, billing, and settlement systems?
- Which functions can be advisory only, and which would require formal operational authority?
- What failure behaviour is acceptable when communications or platform services are unavailable?

### 4.4 County Governments and Local Public Institutions

Potential roles:

- support local pilot identification where energy infrastructure, public facilities, industrial parks, markets, hospitals, water systems, or public-service loads are relevant;
- coordinate local development, resilience, public-service, and economic-impact objectives;
- provide local stakeholder access and implementation support.

Validation required:

- Which county-level functions are relevant to pilot selection and public-service value?
- How should county participation align with national energy-sector authority?
- Which local facilities or infrastructure sites could provide credible pilot environments?

### 4.5 Commercial, Industrial and Institutional Facilities

Potential roles:

- serve as early Facility EMS pilot sites;
- provide real operating data, asset inventory, cost baselines, and operational constraints;
- validate whether energy coordination improves cost, reliability, visibility, maintenance, or sustainability outcomes;
- demonstrate repeatable facility-level value before regional or national scaling.

Validation required:

- Which facilities have sufficient asset complexity to justify a pilot?
- Which assets can be safely monitored or controlled?
- Who authorizes access, data use, and operational changes?
- What baseline will measure value?

### 4.6 Distributed Energy Resource Owners and Operators

Potential roles:

- own or operate solar PV, batteries, backup generators, EV chargers, controllable loads, mini-grids, or other flexible assets;
- provide asset capability, availability, operating constraints, consent, and performance data;
- participate in coordination only within agreed technical, commercial, and legal boundaries.

Validation required:

- Who owns each asset and who may authorize digital access?
- What warranties, safety rules, interconnection conditions, and commercial constraints apply?
- How can participation be revoked or limited?

### 4.7 Technology Providers and System Integrators

Potential roles:

- supply devices, gateways, software components, connectivity, integration services, cybersecurity tools, cloud infrastructure, and support services;
- contribute to open standards, adapters, reference implementations, testing, and deployment methods;
- support interoperability and conformance testing.

Validation required:

- Which components should remain vendor-neutral?
- Which interfaces should be open or standards-based?
- What certification or conformance process is required before integration into critical workflows?

### 4.8 Universities, Research Institutions and Technical Communities

Potential roles:

- support research, modelling, pilots, measurement, evaluation, standards, training, and independent technical review;
- contribute to open-source development and local technical capacity;
- validate claims through reproducible evidence.

Validation required:

- Which research partners can support pilots and evaluation?
- What data can be shared for research, and under what governance?
- Which technical standards and curricula should be developed locally?

### 4.9 Development Finance, Donors and Strategic Funders

Potential roles:

- fund discovery, research, pilots, capacity building, infrastructure readiness, or public-interest components;
- support long-term financing architecture where national or regional infrastructure is justified;
- require safeguards, measurable outcomes, procurement discipline, and sustainability plans.

Validation required:

- Which costs are research, which are commercial, and which are public infrastructure?
- What funding is appropriate for each stage?
- What post-funding operating model prevents grant dependency?

### 4.10 Consumers, Communities and Public Interest

Potential roles:

- benefit from improved reliability, transparency, consumer protection, cost efficiency, renewable integration, and public-value outcomes;
- require privacy, fairness, safety, access, grievance, and accountability protections.

Validation required:

- Which consumer protections apply?
- How will personal, commercial, and operational data be protected?
- How will public benefits be measured and reported?

## 5. Functional Authority Map

| Function | Likely Authority Source | Validation Required |
|---|---|---|
| Policy sponsorship | National policy institutions | Identify official sponsor or convening mechanism. |
| Regulatory approval | Energy regulator and statutory framework | Determine approval, licensing, and reporting requirements. |
| Grid connection and operation | Utility, system, transmission, or distribution functions | Define technical and operational interface rules. |
| Facility access | Facility owner or authorized operator | Confirm site permission and operational boundaries. |
| Asset monitoring | Asset owner, facility operator, contract, or regulation | Define data access and privacy controls. |
| Remote control | Explicit owner/operator authorization plus applicable technical and legal controls | Establish safety, audit, fallback, and revocation rules. |
| Aggregation | Contractual and regulatory authority | Confirm market, utility, and participant requirements. |
| Settlement evidence | Metering, billing, market, regulatory, or contractual rules | Validate admissibility and audit requirements. |
| Cybersecurity requirements | Critical-infrastructure, data-protection, contractual, and internal controls | Define minimum security baseline before connection. |
| Public funding or procurement | Government, utility, donor, or development-finance rules | Define procurement, safeguards, and accountability. |

## 7. Immediate Stakeholder Questions

1. Who should be the official first reviewer of the programme concept?
2. Which institution should validate the problem statement?
3. Which facility or bounded network environment is credible for the first pilot?
4. Which regulatory questions must be resolved before live field integration?
5. Which data can be collected during discovery?
6. Who must approve any control function?
7. What evidence would make the programme worth continuing?
