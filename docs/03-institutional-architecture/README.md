# Institutional Architecture

This section explains the institution required to build, govern, operate, finance, and sustain the proposed energy coordination infrastructure.

## Institutional Definition

The initiative requires more than technology. It requires an institution capable of:

- defining mission and strategy;
- building and maintaining interoperable products;
- operating live infrastructure responsibly;
- managing finance and capital;
- governing risk, legal obligations, cybersecurity, and assurance;
- coordinating with government, utilities, regulators, development partners, customers, and technical communities;
- learning from evidence and improving over time.

## Source Architecture

The detailed institutional architecture is maintained in [organization-docs](../../organization-docs/), which contains 20 domains:

1. Foundation: vision, founder thesis, and growth framework.
2. Product and market: portfolio, roadmap, and business strategy.
3. Operating institution: operating model, finance, risk, and people.
4. Infrastructure and governance: technology, legal, regulatory, and institutional architecture.
5. Ecosystem and public infrastructure: partnerships, resilience, learning, research, markets, national deployment, and master operating system.

## Current Institutional Status

The 20-domain organizational architecture is a **baseline blueprint**. It defines what a mature institution may require. It does not mean every capability, role, control, register, or process already exists.
